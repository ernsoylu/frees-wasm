# D7 — Auxiliary property grids (`FRAUX1`), and the bundle budget they break

**Status:** implemented
**Date:** 2026-08-06
**Supersedes nothing.** Extends [D1](0001-property-backend.md).

## The problem

D1 chose precomputed phase-split `(P,h)` tables as the browser build's
real-fluid property source, and they have served every steam and refrigerant
document in the corpus. But the geometry D1 chose is built out of a saturation
dome and stores exactly three outputs — `T`, `Dmass`, `Smass`. Four things a
document routinely asks for fall outside it, and together they were the whole of
the long-running "real-fluid coverage the tables do not have" divergence
(Phase 5 item 9, restated in every status doc since):

1. **Incompressibles.** `INCOMP::MEG[x]` / `INCOMP::MPG[x]` — what an `EG50`
   coolant loop is written in — have no dome at all.
2. **Fluids with no table.** `R1234yf`, the automotive refrigerant.
3. **Air.** Not tabulated, and `htc_extair` needs its transport.
4. **Transport properties.** `viscosity`, `conductivity`, `Cpmass` — absent for
   *every* fluid, including the two that were tabulated. `htc_evap('R134a', …)`
   failed for this reason alone.

The visible symptom was that `ev-thermal-management`, the catalog's flagship
Systems example, failed at `Block 3 (89 equations): no property table for fluid
'INCOMP::MEG[0.50]'` — and would have failed three more times behind that.

## The decision

Add **one** new artifact kind — `FRAUX1`, a rectangular grid of named outputs
over two axes — generated by `tools/aux-gen` from the same native CoolProp
oracle `tools/table-gen` uses, and consult it from the same `TableBackend`. Its
three uses share one format, one reader and one interpolator:

| Kind | Axes | Outputs | Serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| `INCOMPRESSIBLE` | mass fraction × normalized T | `Dmass`, `Cpmass`, `viscosity`, `conductivity`, `Hmass`, `Smass`, `dHmass_dP`, `dSmass_dP` | the glycols |
| `PRESSURE_TEMPERATURE` | ln P × T | `viscosity`, `conductivity`, `Cpmass`, `Dmass` | air |
| `SATURATION_LINE` | ln P × Q∈{0,1} | `viscosity`, `conductivity`, `Cpmass` | transport on the dome |

Plus an ordinary `tools/table-gen` run for the missing `r1234yf.phtab`.

### Three things that made this much cheaper than it looked

**Transport is only ever asked for *on* the dome.** `htc_evap`, `htc_cond`,
`htc_liquid_only` and `dp_2phase` — every two-phase correlation in the toolkit —
look up `viscosity` / `conductivity` / `Cpmass` at `(P, Q=0)` and `(P, Q=1)` and
nowhere else. So saturated transport is a pair of 1-D arrays over the existing
saturation grid: **~14 KB per fluid**, not the ~256 KB a second full 2-D plane
set would have cost. This is the single reason gap 4 was affordable.

**Incompressibles are exact in pressure.** CoolProp's incompressible model makes
`rho`, `cp`, `mu` and `k` *exactly* pressure-independent and `h`/`s` *exactly
linear* in pressure. Measured, not assumed: `dh/dP` reproduces to all 16 digits
from 1 bar to 100 bar, and `AuxGen` re-checks the linearity at a third pressure
at every node and **fails the run** rather than write a table that quietly is not
the library. Storing `h`/`s` at a reference pressure plus their constant slopes
reproduces CoolProp with no error beyond the `(x, tau)` interpolation.

**The concentration axis can be exact.** `resolveFluid` can only produce a
two-decimal mass fraction — it parses an integer percent and formats it as
`"0.%02d"`, so `EG50` is `0.50` and the language has no spelling for `0.505`.
Putting a node on every value a document can name makes that lookup an exact hit.
This was not a micro-optimisation: 25 columns over `[0, 0.6]` measured viscosity
at `3.2e-2`, refining only as `1/n_x` — first-order, the signature of
interpolating across the nonlinear freeze curve. Exact nodes dropped the same
measurement to `1.3e-3` and restored clean second-order convergence.

## Measured error

Worst `max_rel` over 4000 random interior states per grid, against CoolProp
8.0.0 (`fixtures/auxtables/AUX-ERROR-REPORT.json`):

| Grid | bytes | `Dmass` | `Cpmass` | `viscosity` | `conductivity` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `INCOMP::MEG` 61×48 | 94 820 | 1.6e-5 | 9.5e-5 | 1.3e-3 | 6.6e-5 |
| `INCOMP::MPG` 61×48 | 94 820 | 1.5e-5 | 1.4e-5 | 9.1e-4 | 1.4e-4 |
| `Air` 24×64 | 25 072 | 1.9e-4 | 2.2e-4 | 1.2e-4 | 1.4e-4 |
| `Water` sat 512×2 | 14 480 | — | 8.2e-4 | 6.5e-5 | 4.1e-4 |
| `R134a` sat 512×2 | 14 480 | — | 5.3e-4 | 7.0e-5 | 1.7e-4 |
| `R1234yf` sat 512×2 | 14 488 | — | 1.6e-3 | 2.2e-4 | 6.2e-4 |

The incompressible grids' normalized-temperature axis is placed by
**equidistributing the interpolation error of `ln(mu)`** rather than uniformly,
which costs nothing (the axis is written out and binary-searched) and was worth
a measurable amount: `ev-thermal-management` graded `4.427e-3` with a guessed
`u²` spacing and `8.951e-4` with the computed one.

`r1234yf.phtab` measures in D1's own class: `T` 9.3e-5, `Dmass` 1.3e-3 (vapor),
`Smass` 6.3e-5.

**Viscosity and saturated `Cpmass` are the two outliers, and they are stated
rather than smoothed over.** They feed correlations whose own Nusselt and
two-phase multipliers are ±20 %-accurate by construction, so a 1.6e-3 property
is three orders of magnitude inside the correlation's own error. Everything a
state calculation uses directly — `Dmass`, `Hmass`, `Smass` — is at or below
2.2e-4, i.e. D1's class.

### The one place that error is amplified, and why it is not a table defect

`ev-thermal-management` grades at `8.951e-4` worst-variable, and all three of its
worst variables descend from one call:
`htc_1phase('EG50', 200 kPa, 290 K, 0.23, 0.008, 1.5e-4)`. Its Reynolds number at
that state is **2987 — dead centre of `nuSinglePhase`'s 2300..4000
laminar↔turbulent blend**, where Nu sweeps 3.66 → ~30. A ~5e-4 viscosity error
lands on the steepest part of the correlation and comes out near 9e-4.

The *operating point* chooses that gain, not the table: the same grid gives
`1.310e-4` on `sysdesign-ex11-liquid-cooling-loop`, which sits off the blend.
The median over all 229 variables of the EV document is `1.0e-6`.

## Consequences

**Two fixtures promoted** — `ev-thermal-management` and
`sysdesign-ex11-liquid-cooling-loop` — with measured tolerances recorded in
`fixtures/tolerances.json` per its own rules. Corpus 702 → **704**, all passing.

**The bundle budget is broken, and this is the cost to weigh.**

```
   before   2944.0 KiB raw / 1390.0 KiB gzipped
   after    3345.1 KiB raw / 1810.5 KiB gzipped
   budget   3072.0 KiB
            108.9 % of budget — OVER by 273.1 KiB
```

The added bytes are almost entirely data: `r1234yf.phtab` 236 KB (59 % of the
increase), the two glycol grids 185 KB, air 24 KB, three saturation grids 43 KB.
Compression is **not** a lever — these are f32 grids of smooth functions and
gzip only reaches 0.87–0.94 on them, which is why the gzipped figure rose almost
as much as the raw one.

There is no arrangement of these four gaps that fits in the 128 KiB of headroom
that existed. The options, measured:

| Option | Saves | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Drop `MPG` (leaves `PG30` unserved) | 90 KiB | 3255 KiB — still over |
| Regenerate R1234yf at 96×48 | ~132 KiB | 3213 KiB — still over, and coarser |
| Both, plus glycol `ntau` 48→32 | ~252 KiB | 3093 KiB — still over |
| Raise the budget | — | the only option that actually closes |

**The budget should move to 3456 KiB.** The precompiled tables *are* the
product's differentiator — offline real-fluid properties in a browser tab — and
the alternative D1 named (lazily fetching tables) is the thing Phase 11's
offline-first PWA proof was built to avoid. `install_from_bytes` remains the
runtime seam for anyone who wants to make the opposite trade; it now installs
aux grids alongside split tables, so a fetched artifact still works.

This is recorded as a **breach to be ratified, not a fait accompli**: the number
above is the honest measurement, and shrinking the shipped set back under
3072 KiB is a one-line change to `BUILTIN_TABLES` / `BUILTIN_AUX` if the budget
is judged the more important constraint.

## What this does not close

`hx-correlations-fluid` remains blocked. It calls `htc_1phase('Water', P, T)` —
**single-phase** transport off the saturation line, which none of the three grid
kinds covers. The path is now obvious and cheap: a fourth use of the existing
`PRESSURE_TEMPERATURE` kind, ragged in T so the liquid piece does not
interpolate across the dome. It was left out because it is not what the reported
failure needed, and because it would add to a bundle that is already over.

`thermo-compliance` is still blocked on `CompressibilityFactor`, unchanged.
